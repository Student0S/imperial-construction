Project Anatomy

From compartmentalised Victorian rooms to an architectural sanctuary.

1. The Challenge

The homeowners, a family of five living in a semi-detached Victorian villa, loved the leafy setting of Chislehurst but had completely outgrown their layout. The ground floor was divided into three dark, disconnected rooms with an isolated rear galley kitchen cut off from both natural light and the garden patio.

The primary structural challenge was removing two central load-bearing walls across an 8-metre span while supporting two upper storeys of solid London brickwork without visible ceiling downstands.

2. Engineering & Design Solution

Imperial's structural engineering team designed an integrated three-way steel picture frame (flitch beams and concealed goalpost steelwork) hoisted into place using specialist hydraulic gear. This eliminated all downstands, delivering an uninterrupted 2.8m ceiling height throughout.

Key architectural specifications included:

  • Glazing: Floor-to-ceiling ultra-slim Crittall-style aluminium doors with thermal break coatings.
  • Rooflights: Frameless structural solar-control glass roof lantern flooding the central dining island with southern daylight.
  • Joinery: Hand-crafted bespoke shaker cabinetry finished in Little Greene Obsidian Green with solid brass unlacquered pull handles.
  • Flooring: Prime grade brushed European oak herringbone over multi-zone hydronic underfloor heating.

3. The Handover & Results

The extension added 65 square metres of prime usable family space. Completed within exactly 14 weeks on the agreed fixed-price contract, with zero snagging disputes and full Building Control certification handed over on day one.
